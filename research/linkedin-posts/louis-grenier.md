# Louis Grenier – LinkedIn Posts

## Post 1 – [Three weeks ago]

[Link to original post] ( https://www.linkedin.com/posts/louisgrenier_but-none-of-our-competitors-are-doing-it-share-7462905293283405826-mwrs/?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAEgnkiABZdlJeQGmC1R-m2Hd8MQmVjYK_os)


“But NONE of our competitors are doing it!!! That’s so f*cking great!!!”

—Junior marketer me, a decade ago.

You see, young and naive Louis thought competitor absence was ALWAYS a good thing.

“They don’t have this feature, that’s great news, let’s build it.

They don’t use this colour, that’s great news, let’s use it.

They don’t serve this persona, that’s great news, let’s do something for them.”

But now, after working with so many b2b tech companies, I’ve learned that this thinking can either make a product stand the fuck out… or make it blend the f*ck in so much, it becomes as beige as my best pair of Nike Air. 

Here’s the nuance I’ve learned: you can’t apply the same rule to two completely different things.

(1) If you’re picking a problem to solve, and no competitor is touching it, especially a big one, the default read for me is: “Hmmm this is not great news. This probably means that problem is not painful.” 

That’s just differentiation for the sake of it. No one cares if you introduce NEW features no one else has if those features solve problems that don’t exist.

The only thing that flips that read is insight foraging evidence. A real ignored struggle you can point to.

In most b2b markets, especially crowded ones, the ignored struggles you’ll find are small, specific, and relevant to a small addressable market. It’s fine to solve them, but don’t expect to revolutionise your industry.

However…

(2) If you’re picking a brand asset (a set of colors, shapes, a sound, a character, a mascot, et ) and no competitor is using it? The default read is, “F*ck yeah!! That’s great news.”

This means there’s room for distinctiveness (not differentiation). You have a shot at associating your brand with memory structures that no other competitors have.

And the good thing compared to ignored struggles? Distinctive assets don’t have to matter to customers. They can be random and meaning-free.

For example, we’ve developed a mascot for a PR software: it’s a little green harvest mouse called Pierre. What are the chances that any of their direct or even indirect competitors will be using a similar asset? Practically ZERO.

——

Anyhoo… That’s what I would tell my younger marketer self so he doesn’t embarrass himself in meetings. Also, I’d tell him to invest in NVIDIA stock."

## Post 3 – [Four weeks ago]

[Link to original post] (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/louisgrenier_i-finally-have-a-contrarian-take-on-claude-activity-7461077994292469760-2B5L?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAEgnkiABZdlJeQGmC1R-m2Hd8MQmVjYK_os)

"I finally have a contrarian take on Claude Code, especially for marketers:

You know those pre-made/plug-and-play skills, agents, workflows, blah blah blah… you can add to your setup in two clicks?

Useless.

U-SE-LESS.

U👏S👏E👏L👏E👏S👏S.

Sure, some of them may inspire you for your own setup, but, after spending months building out my AI “co-pilot”, I can confidently say they are like canned mushrooms… blend, boring, just blehhh.

The best way to get the most out of YOUR AI setup is to talk to it the way you would talk to an all-around smart co-pilot with deep knowledge, who can search for things on your behalf, recommend the best course of action, set things up, test them, learn from its mistakes, adjust course…

Overtime, you will build things that fit around your work and life like a GLOVE. Like organic porcini mushrooms you’ve handpicked from the nearby forest with your own two hands.

Oh sure, you might miss out on a few tricks and hacks and whatever… but in the grand scheme of things, those don’t matter at all.

It’s the same first-principle thinking I advocate for in marketing: focus on the things that will never, ever change. Sure, AI tools evolve faster than we can possibly keep up with, but there are foundations that you can rely on.

Don’t get bamboozled."
