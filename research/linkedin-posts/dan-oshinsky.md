## Post 1 – [a month ago]

[Link to original post] (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/danoshinsky_im-making-it-my-personal-mission-for-2026-activity-7459614559834648576-UK5O?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAEgnkiABZdlJeQGmC1R-m2Hd8MQmVjYK_os)

"I’m making it my personal mission for 2026 to eliminate one word from every email person’s vocabulary:

The word? E-blast.

When someone tells me they’re working on an “e-blast,” what they’re really saying is: We just send an email out to everyone, regardless of whether they might want it!

You can do so much better.

Collect data! Set up segments based on that data! Then target the readers you really want to reach with a message that might resonate with them!

Think about email as a 1-1 channel. It’s a chance to reach that one specific reader for whom this message with really resonate.

The sooner you do — and the sooner you put those e-blasts in the past — the sooner you’ll see great results."

## Post 2 – [a month ago]

[Link to original post] (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/danoshinsky_how-do-you-get-the-attention-of-people-you-activity-7452001885626220544-DEOs?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAEgnkiABZdlJeQGmC1R-m2Hd8MQmVjYK_os)

"How do you get the attention of people you admire? Here’s a simple one that’s worked well for me over the years: 

1. Sign up for their newsletter.

2. When they write something great, hit reply to the newsletter and tell them. 

3. Don’t ask for anything; just tell them you loved what they wrote.

Even the biggest newsletter writers you know get fewer email replies than you think. When readers show up and genuinely want to start a conversation, you’ll find that many of these writers are surprisingly open to it. Over time, as you build a relationship, you might be able to discuss partnership opportunities: Cross-promotion, guest posting, collaborating on events or lead magnets.

Hit reply. You never know what might happen next."
