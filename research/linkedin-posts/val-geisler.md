## Post 1 – [a month ago]

[Link to original post] (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lovevalgeisler_your-next-best-hire-is-an-entrepreneur-looking-activity-7459969453292945408-F_Yq?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAEgnkiABZdlJeQGmC1R-m2Hd8MQmVjYK_os)

"Your next best hire is an entrepreneur looking to make a pivot, but you probably won't hire them.

You won't hire them because they don't have the exact job title.

You won't hire them because their years of experience in your specific field doesn't match what your job description says.

You won't hire them because you're focused on labels, not skills. 

But if you really look at entrepreneurs, they're your secret weapon.

They ran a business which means they've done sales, they've done marketing, they've project managed, they've developed products, they've done their own accounting and they've hired and fired people. 

And that's just what it takes to run a business on the surface. It doesn't even begin to touch what they've spent years mastering.

The freelance copywriter who has been booking out clients 9 months in advance is clearly a master at conversion, seeing as he wrote his own sales pages. Put him on product marketing and watch your landing pages convert faster than ever before.

That designer you brought in for a project would be a resourceful full time hire, her ability to flex into multiple different clients at once shows she can leverage her time and talent in a way most others struggle with. She's shine as a creative director.

The bookkeeper who reminds her clients about missing receipts and questionable line items is a true stickler for details, pretty important for a future project manager or head of finance.

Too often we're optimizing for titles. Paying attention to keywords over key skills. 

The talent is out there, you're just looking in the wrong places."

## Post 2 – [a month ago]

[Link to original post] (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lovevalgeisler_every-brand-says-they-talk-to-their-customers-activity-7455348297151647744-1ad0?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAEgnkiABZdlJeQGmC1R-m2Hd8MQmVjYK_os)

"Every brand says they talk to their customers, but talking and listening are two different things.

And both have good and bad sides so you have to really know what you're doing and why you're doing it.

😔 Bad talking: Taking up 75% of the talk time during a QBR. Customer tunes out.
😁 Good talking: Segmented messaging for new product releases, because you kept track of feature requests and can personalize updates around that request.

😔 Bad listening: Running surveys and taking attribution quotes at face value.
😁 Good listening: Getting on calls with customers and asking questions that get you to the real answer.

Good talking and good listening aren't hard to do, but they take time and intention and tbh not everyone puts in the work to actually be good at it. 

You need reps. You need to get on calls with customers and fumble a few. You need to refine your question list and hone your willingness to not use that list and just have a conversation.

Good talking and good listening = good messaging.

It's good to put the time in to getting good."

## Post 3 – [a month ago]

[Link to original post] (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lovevalgeisler_one-thing-of-many-that-i-admire-about-blake-activity-7450193760975482880-KSCk?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAEgnkiABZdlJeQGmC1R-m2Hd8MQmVjYK_os)

"One thing (of many) that I admire about Blake Imperl is that he gives his contractors and agency partners credit. I've seen this done the opposite way and here's how it goes:

Proudly, leadership will say "We have a team of 30 people and we're scaling profit without growing the team," meanwhile there's a steadily rising number of contractors on the roster who "don't count" as team members.

Frankly, it's demoralizing. If you don't care to treat your contractors as part of your team, when they are acting as part of your team, what does that say about you as a leader? 

When Blake talks about his team, he includes the fractional/agency/contractor support. Why? Because they are part of the team. Blake actively manages them, they meet regularly, they have deliverables and metrics they're held accountable to.

Your agency and contractor partners are part of your success, treat them as part of the team."
